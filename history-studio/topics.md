# Topic ideas

From the guide's Prompt 2 (topic finder). Written for the stitched tapestry
look; every idea has a place to map, so they suit any of the four looks.
Ranked by how surprising the hook is. Each has a source to start from.

1. **The Army That Walked Across the Sea** (35 chars)
   - Hook: How did an army invade Denmark by marching across the sea?
   - Place and year: the Little and Great Belts, Denmark, January–February 1658
   - Why it mattered: Charles X Gustav's Swedes crossed the frozen straits and forced the Treaty of Roskilde. Denmark lost Scania, Halland and Blekinge, which are still southern Sweden today.
   - Start with: the Treaty of Roskilde (1658)

2. **The Pope Who Put a Corpse on Trial** (34 chars)
   - Hook: Why did a pope dig up his predecessor and put him on trial?
   - Place and year: Rome, January 897
   - Why it mattered: the Cadaver Synod was the low point of papal politics. Pope Stephen VI was overthrown and strangled within months.
   - Start with: Liutprand of Cremona, *Antapodosis*

3. **The Solar Storm That Dated Vikings in America** (45 chars)
   - Hook: How do we know Vikings were cutting wood in America in exactly 1021?
   - Place and year: L'Anse aux Meadows, Newfoundland, 1021
   - Why it mattered: Europeans reached the Americas almost 500 years before Columbus. A solar storm in 993 left a marker in tree rings that dates the cuts to the year.
   - Start with: Kuitems et al., *Nature* (2021)

4. **The Emperor Who Lost His Nose and Came Back** (43 chars)
   - Hook: How did an emperor whose nose was cut off take back his throne?
   - Place and year: Constantinople and Cherson (Crimea), 695–705
   - Why it mattered: Justinian II was mutilated so he could never rule again. He returned ten years later with a Bulgar army. The golden nose of legend is probably a myth.
   - Start with: Theophanes the Confessor, *Chronographia*

5. **The War Over a Wooden Bucket** (28 chars)
   - Hook: Did two Italian cities really go to war over a stolen bucket?
   - Place and year: Zappolino, near Bologna, 1325
   - Why it mattered: the bucket was only the spark in a long Guelph–Ghibelline feud, but the battle was real. Modena won and still keeps the bucket.
   - Start with: Alessandro Tassoni, *La secchia rapita* (1622), then the chronicles behind it

6. **The Warship Built During a King's Dinner** (40 chars)
   - Hook: Could Venice really build a warship while a king ate dinner?
   - Place and year: the Venetian Arsenal, 1574
   - Why it mattered: according to accounts of Henry III of France's visit, the Arsenal assembled a galley during his banquet. It was assembly-line production centuries before factories, and it powered Venice's navy.
   - Start with: Venetian accounts of Henry III's visit, July 1574

7. **The Children's Crusade That Wasn't** (34 chars)
   - Hook: Was the Children's Crusade even made up of children?
   - Place and year: from Cologne and Vendôme to the Mediterranean, 1212
   - Why it mattered: the chroniclers' word *pueri* may have meant poor young people, not children. That retells one of the Middle Ages' most famous stories.
   - Start with: Gary Dickson, *The Children's Crusade* (2008)

8. **Viking Graffiti in Hagia Sophia** (31 chars)
   - Hook: Why are there Viking runes carved inside Hagia Sophia?
   - Place and year: Constantinople, 9th century
   - Why it mattered: "Halfdan" scratched his name in the gallery. Norsemen travelled the rivers to Byzantium and became the emperor's Varangian Guard in 988.
   - Start with: the Halfdan inscription in the south gallery

9. **The King's World Map Drawn Upside Down** (38 chars)
   - Hook: Why did a Christian king hire a Muslim scholar to map the world with south on top?
   - Place and year: Palermo, Sicily, 1154
   - Why it mattered: al-Idrisi's map for Roger II was among the most accurate world maps for centuries.
   - Start with: the Tabula Rogeriana

10. **The Forgotten Fourth Superpower** (31 chars)
    - Hook: Which African kingdom did a Persian prophet rank beside Rome and China?
    - Place and year: Aksum, Ethiopia, 3rd century
    - Why it mattered: Aksum controlled Red Sea trade and minted its own gold coins. It became one of the first Christian states under Ezana.
    - Start with: the *Kephalaia* attributed to Mani

11. **The King Who Wrote to Stop the Slave Trade** (42 chars)
    - Hook: Why did a Christian king in Africa beg Portugal to stop the slave trade?
    - Place and year: Mbanza Kongo, Kingdom of Kongo, 1526
    - Why it mattered: Afonso I's letters to João III are among the earliest written protests against the Atlantic slave trade, from inside Africa.
    - Start with: Afonso I's letters to João III (1526)

12. **The Lost Viking Voyage Carved in Stone** (38 chars)
    - Hook: Why do about 26 Swedish runestones mourn men who sailed to the Caspian?
    - Place and year: Lake Mälaren, Sweden, to Serkland, about 1041
    - Why it mattered: Ingvar the Far-Travelled led the last great Viking expedition east, and almost no one came home.
    - Start with: the Ingvar runestones; *Yngvars saga víðförla*

13. **Why the Greenland Vikings Disappeared** (37 chars)
    - Hook: Why did the Norse of Greenland vanish after more than 400 years?
    - Place and year: the Eastern Settlement, Greenland, about 1450
    - Why it mattered: walrus ivory was their main export. When Europe stopped wanting it and the climate cooled, the colony faded.
    - Start with: ancient-DNA studies of medieval walrus ivory (2018–2020)

14. **The Battle That Supposedly Brought Paper West** (45 chars)
    - Hook: Did one battle carry the secret of paper to the Islamic world?
    - Place and year: the Talas River, Central Asia, 751
    - Why it mattered: it was the only battle between the Abbasid Caliphate and Tang China. The famous paper story is probably exaggerated, which is the twist.
    - Start with: Du Huan's *Jingxingji* (a Tang soldier captured at Talas)

15. **America's Lost City on the Mississippi** (38 chars)
    - Hook: How did a city of thousands rise and vanish on the Mississippi?
    - Place and year: Cahokia, near today's St. Louis, about 1050–1350
    - Why it mattered: it was the largest city north of Mexico, possibly as big as London at the time, and it was abandoned before Europeans arrived.
    - Start with: excavations at Monks Mound and Cahokia Mounds

Left out as overdone: Lindisfarne itself, Pompeii, the fall of Rome, the
Titanic, Mansa Musa's gold, the dancing plague, the Great Emu War, and the
guide's own example ideas.
